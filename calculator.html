Cost Segregation Calculator: Test the Assumptions

Compare a bonus-depreciation illustration using amounts and percentages you supply. This calculator does not infer a reclassification percentage from the property type, select the applicable tax rules, or determine whether you can use a deduction.

Enter a supported scenario

Use dollars for amounts and percentages from 0 to 100. Depreciable basis must exclude land and amounts outside the analysis. The eligible portion is a user assumption requiring professional review. Complete every field, entering 0 only when confirmed. Do not submit names, addresses or tax identifiers. This worksheet processes inputs locally and does not save them.

Enter your assumptions or load the fictional scenario to begin.

How the calculation works

  1. Modeled bonus deduction = assumed eligible portion multiplied by the entered bonus rate.
  2. Assumed usable deduction = modeled bonus deduction multiplied by the current-use percentage you entered.
  3. Gross illustrative federal tax effect = assumed usable deduction multiplied by your entered marginal federal rate.
  4. The final line subtracts your quoted fee from that gross effect. It is an arithmetic comparison, not an incremental return on the study.

Dollar outputs are rounded to cents at each stage. Ordinary depreciation, a no-study comparison, state taxes, other tax interactions, financing, future recapture and the timing value of money are not modeled. You would need those items, plus property-specific analysis, to evaluate whether commissioning a study is worthwhile.

Fictional example: test reduced deduction use

Assume $800,000 of depreciable basis excluding land, a $200,000 eligible portion, a 60% bonus rate, 50% current deduction use, a 37% federal rate and a $2,500 fee. These are teaching inputs, not a typical property result or a determination of the applicable bonus rate.

The modeled bonus deduction is $120,000. The assumed usable amount is $60,000. Multiplying by 37% gives a gross illustrative federal effect of $22,200; subtracting the entered fee leaves $19,700. That last figure does not establish savings compared with depreciation otherwise available. Try changing current use to 0% to see why a large allocation does not necessarily create an immediate benefit.

Why there is no automatic property-type percentage

The eligible amount needs support from the actual property and asset analysis. A purchase price can include land and separately recorded assets, and a study may cover only part of an acquisition or a later improvement. Do not substitute an industry percentage for the report's cost reconciliation. Use the report reconciliation worksheet to organize arithmetic questions before the professional review.

Why purchase year alone does not select a bonus rate

The IRS Form 4562 instructions describe 100% bonus depreciation for certain qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025, alongside different treatment for certain earlier acquisitions. Asset eligibility, acquisition rules, service dates and elections matter. The calculator intentionally asks you to enter a rate confirmed for your scenario; it does not determine eligibility from a single year dropdown. Consult IRS Publication 946 and your preparer for the applicable analysis.
