Existing-Property Cost Segregation: Review Form 3115 and Catch-Up Adjustments

An existing property can be reviewed for cost segregation after acquisition, but a study does not automatically entitle its owner to a catch-up deduction. First determine whether the depreciation treatment is incorrect, whether a method of accounting has been adopted, and which correction or consent procedure applies.

This guide separates the study, the method-change decision, the opening adjustment, and the return’s ability to use a deduction. Download the blank existing-property review record and fictional signed-adjustment examples. They do not establish actual depreciation, eligibility, an election, or tax savings.

Start with the asset and return history

Collect the acquisition and improvement records, land allocation, placed-in-service evidence, original depreciation schedules, Forms 4562, prior returns, and any bonus, section 179, ADS, or other relevant elections. Reconcile assets added, disposed of, or transferred. A newly written study cannot replace missing historical facts or justify a standard reclassification percentage.

Reconstruct the treatment under the rules applicable to each asset and its relevant acquisition and placed-in-service dates. Do not apply today’s bonus percentage retroactively merely because the study is completed today. Review the method, recovery period, convention, eligible basis, and elections for each affected item. The first-year timing comparison illustrates why dividing basis by a full recovery period is not enough for a first-year deduction.

Distinguish an error correction from a method change

IRS Publication 946 explains that mathematical and posting errors can have an amended-return correction path. A depreciation method generally is adopted by using a permissible method on the first return, or the same impermissible method on two or more consecutively filed returns, with exceptions under the applicable guidance. The facts and procedural rules determine the correction route; the age of the property alone does not.

Certain depreciation-method changes require IRS consent under section 446(e). Some qualify for automatic procedures; others require nonautomatic consent or a different correction. Changes to basis, placed-in-service facts, elections, ownership, or disposed assets can have separate rules and exclusions. “Automatic” does not mean that any completed study qualifies or that sending a form approves the underlying classifications.

Review Revenue Procedure 2025-23, as modified or superseded, including the relevant depreciation provisions, together with the general procedures and current Form 3115 instructions. Confirm the designated change number and scope for the actual year of change. Do not assume a designation, exception, or filing window from another taxpayer’s example.

Compute the opening adjustment with the correct sign

For a qualifying depreciation change using section 481(a), the adjustment generally compares cumulative depreciation taken into account under the present method with cumulative depreciation allowable under the proposed method for years before the year of change. It prevents amounts from being duplicated or omitted. The year-of-change depreciation calculation is separate.

Use the exact computation required by the applicable procedure, including any modified adjustment or cut-off rule. The examples below simply assume eligible cumulative inputs and the general adjustment periods. They are not actual MACRS schedules and do not establish that any taxpayer qualifies for those periods.

The Form 3115 instructions describe general periods of one year for a negative adjustment and four years for a positive adjustment. Elections, special procedures, examination status, business cessation, and other circumstances can change the result. All three examples leave actual allowable deduction, tax effect, and filing eligibility unresolved.

Prevent double counting and preserve the audit trail

Reconcile the existing asset schedule to the proposed schedule before preparing the opening adjustment. Keep original total basis, land, component allocations, prior allowed or allowable depreciation, and remaining basis distinguishable. An amount already deducted cannot simply be deducted again because a new report describes it differently.

Separate the opening section 481(a) adjustment from current-year depreciation, future-year amounts, and any actual tax payment or refund. Tie each figure to assets and years, and explain the proposed legal treatment. A study percentage multiplied by the building value is not a substitute for these schedules.

Confirm filing requirements and limitations

For an automatic change, the general instructions call for the original Form 3115 with the timely filed return for the year of change and a separately submitted signed duplicate under the applicable procedures, unless different instructions apply. Confirm the current address or permitted transmission method, timing, attachments, and additional-copy requirements. The IRS generally does not acknowledge automatic requests, so lack of a response is not evidence that the facts or computation were approved.

Nonautomatic requests follow different timing and consent procedures. Prior changes, a final year, property disposition, examination or issue status, and procedural exclusions can matter. The ability to include open and closed years in a qualifying opening adjustment does not create an unlimited right to file Form 3115 whenever desired, or to claim an amended-return refund outside its applicable rules.

Technical audit protection under a revenue procedure is conditional and limited. It is not a promise that filing cannot lead to examination, that earlier returns are beyond review, or that a study’s classifications will be accepted. Preserve evidence and obtain a specific procedural conclusion.

Review whether the return can use the adjustment

A negative adjustment is not automatically a currently usable loss against wages. Review entity basis, at-risk and passive limitations, personal use, excess-business-loss and other return rules, and how any suspended amount is tracked. Compare study and preparation fees, the supported whole-return tax effect, later depreciation, state treatment, and eventual disposition consequences. Leave a savings estimate unresolved until the required facts are available.

Use the STR classification and participation review when short-stay rules are relevant. A participation conclusion and a method-change eligibility conclusion answer separate questions.

Primary sources and review date

  • IRS Publication 946: correcting depreciation and adopted methods.
  • Form 3115 instructions: request procedures, supporting schedules, adjustment periods, and filing requirements.
  • Revenue Procedure 2025-23: automatic-change scope and depreciation provisions; check subsequent modifications and any successor.
  • Revenue Procedure 2015-13: general consent, adjustment, and audit-protection procedures, as modified.
