Cost Segregation Comparison: First-Year Timing and Usable Deductions

A cost-segregation comparison should answer three questions separately: how an allocation changes depreciation timing, how much deduction the owner can use, and whether the result is worthwhile after costs and sale consequences. A larger first-year depreciation number answers only the first question.

This original fictional example isolates the timing calculation. It is not a client result, a typical reclassification percentage or a recommendation to treat known short-life assets as part of a building. The building-only column is a simplified preliminary projection to reconcile against a completed allocation, not an alternative reporting election.

Write down the assumptions before calculating

  • Calendar-year taxpayer; $500,000 total acquisition basis, with a separately supported $100,000 land allocation and $400,000 depreciable basis. Assume no other acquisition-basis adjustments for this illustration.
  • Acquired in March 2026 and placed in service in July 2026; entirely business use. The retained building qualifies as residential rental property under 27.5-year GDS straight-line treatment. Assume no ADS requirement or other rule changing that treatment.
  • A hypothetical, independently supported allocation identifies $120,000 of qualified assets eligible for 100% bonus depreciation and leaves $280,000 in the building. No election out, Section 179 deduction or eligibility restriction is assumed. The $120,000 amount is invented for arithmetic, not inferred from the purchase price.

IRS Publication 946 explains bonus eligibility, recovery periods and conventions. The 100% allowance applies to certain qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025; the report delivery date does not establish eligibility. Residential rental property uses the mid-month convention under these assumed rules. A July service date gives 5.5 months of first-year building depreciation, not a full year.

The first-year timing difference is $124,666.67 − $6,666.67 = $118,000. It is an additional depreciation deduction in this model, not $118,000 of cash or tax savings. Subsequent building deductions are smaller in the allocated column. Actual return software may differ by rounding, and later additions, dispositions or changes in use require further calculations.

Do not copy the $120,000 allocation into another property. Ask the reviewer to connect each asset group to its cost support, classification, service date and treatment. Reconcile the total back to the same $400,000 basis so the comparison does not accidentally create additional basis.

Turn a timing difference into a return comparison

Publication 925 explains basis, at-risk and passive-activity limits, followed where applicable by excess business loss rules. A depreciation deduction can increase a suspended loss instead of reducing current tax. Classification and participation must be evaluated on the owner's facts.

Use two complete return projections with the same income and ownership facts. Record which losses are usable now, which carry forward and which assumptions remain unresolved. Compare federal and state results separately. Do not multiply the entire timing difference by one advertised bracket and describe that number as money available to invest.

  • Tax effect: current-year liability under each reviewed projection, plus the destination of any suspended loss.
  • Costs: the actual study quote, filing work and future record-maintenance obligations.
  • Timing: when tax payments would change and when any carryforward could realistically be used.
  • Decision: identify the missing fact that could reverse the recommendation before committing to a study.

Include a sale scenario before calling the result a benefit

Publication 544 distinguishes Section 1245 recapture, Section 1250 treatment and unrecaptured Section 1250 gain. A recovery-period label alone does not determine the recapture category. The asset allocation, depreciation and sale proceeds all matter. Ordinary-income recapture is not interchangeable with unrecaptured Section 1250 gain.

A like-kind exchange does not automatically eliminate every recapture amount. Review qualifying real property and the actual exchange structure separately; Publication 544 includes circumstances where recapture remains reportable. Model a taxable sale as well as any proposed exchange instead of assuming perpetual deferral.

For your comparison file, keep a copy of each draft, the reviewed final allocation, the depreciation schedule and the reasons the conclusion changed. A longer holding period or an assumed investment return should not substitute for an asset-level exit calculation.

Use the report and FAQ to resolve the next question

Follow the cost-segregation report reading order to check inputs before the deduction summary. The study eligibility and scope FAQ covers questions about prior depreciation, quotes and preparer responsibilities.

This example demonstrates timing under stated assumptions. It does not establish eligibility, a correct asset allocation or a guaranteed after-tax advantage. Bring the model and supporting records to AE Tax Advisors' cost-segregation review for a property-specific engagement.
