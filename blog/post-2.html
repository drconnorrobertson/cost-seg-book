STR Cost Segregation: Check Stay Length, Participation, and Usable Losses

Published August 25, 2026 | Reviewed November 22, 2026

A short booking period, owner work, and a cost segregation study answer different tax questions. None by itself establishes a current deduction against wages. Review the activity classification, qualifying participation, depreciable basis and asset rules, and the owner’s loss limitations before assigning a tax benefit.

Use the blank STR tax review record and fictional stay-and-participation cases to organize the evidence. They do not calculate tax savings or approve a filing position.

First determine the activity’s average customer-use period

The seven-day rental-activity exception is in Temporary Regulation 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A), explained in IRS Publication 925. It is not a blanket exception for listings on Airbnb or another platform. For a simple single-class activity, divide total days in rental periods by the number of rentals during the tax year. More complex activities require the regulation’s customer-use, renewal, and property-class rules.

Four fictional periods of 2, 4, 8, and 14 days total 28 days: 28 ÷ 4 = 7. One stay longer than seven days does not by itself make the average fail. Changing the third period to 9 days gives 29 ÷ 4 = 7.25, which fails this particular exception. Do not divide by available nights, occupied calendar months, or a platform’s advertised maximum stay.

A period above seven days does not settle every classification question. Another exception can apply, including an average of 30 days or less with significant personal services under the relevant rules. Ordinary rental services do not automatically establish significant services. Review the actual operation rather than assuming either result from an average alone.

Then apply the relevant participation test

If the activity falls outside the rental-activity definition, the material-participation rules still need review to determine passive or nonpassive treatment. The rules contain seven tests and qualifications. Two commonly discussed tests are participation for more than 500 hours, and participation for more than 100 hours and at least as much as any other individual. The latter is test 3, not the separate significant-participation-activity test.

For the 100-hour comparison, compare each other person’s qualifying participation with the owner’s, not everyone’s combined hours. Exactly 500 hours does not pass the more-than-500 test. A failed selected test does not prove failure of all seven tests. Limited-partner interests, grouping, spouse participation, prior years, and facts-and-circumstances provisions can require further analysis.

Keep a supportable participation record

Record the date, person, specific work, duration, activity, and supporting booking, message, receipt, or maintenance record. Separate operational work from investor-type monitoring, unsupported estimates, and time that does not qualify under the regulations. Do not count the same interval twice. A timestamped message is evidence of a task, not proof that the entire surrounding day was spent on it.

Timely records are useful, but neither a particular spreadsheet nor a weekly update automatically establishes material participation. The regulations allow proof by reasonable means and explain what participation counts. Obtain support for other individuals’ hours when the chosen test depends on that comparison.

Depreciation eligibility is a separate review

A section 469 short-stay exception does not automatically make every STR building 39-year nonresidential property. Building classification and recovery period follow separate depreciation definitions and facts. Determine the actual use, applicable dwelling-unit and transient-use rules, and method; do not derive a 27.5- or 39-year conclusion solely from the booking average.

Establish acquisition and improvement basis, allocate land separately, avoid counting furnishings or closing costs twice, and document when assets were ready and available for their intended use. Component classifications require factual and legal support; a generic 30%–40% reclassification range is not evidence of a particular property’s result.

Current law generally allows 100% bonus depreciation for eligible qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, subject to the applicable acquisition, use, election, and other requirements. A recovery period of 20 years or less alone does not prove every requirement, and the building itself does not become bonus-eligible because some components qualify. Review older acquisitions, binding contracts, used-property rules, ADS requirements, and elections separately.

The first-year timing and usable-deduction comparison explains why placed-in-service conventions and deduction usability matter. A cost segregation allocation is not itself a refund calculation.

Finish the owner-level loss and cash review

Even a properly computed depreciation amount and nonpassive classification do not automatically produce an immediately deductible loss. Review basis and at-risk limits, personal use and section 280A, passive rules where applicable, excess-business-loss limits, and other return provisions. Track suspended losses by their actual limitation. Nonpassive treatment under section 469 does not by itself decide Schedule C versus Schedule E or self-employment tax.

Compare supported whole-return results with and without the study, including fees, state treatment, later deductions, disposition gain and recapture, and any relevant financing or timing facts. Do not multiply all depreciation by one guessed wage bracket or treat deferral as a permanent saving. A study fee or a market square-foot price is also not established by this article.

Primary sources and review date

  • IRS Publication 925: rental exceptions, material participation, and passive/at-risk limits.
  • Temporary Regulation 1.469-5T: participation tests, qualifying work, and proof.
  • IRS Publication 527: rental-property depreciation and personal use.
  • IRS Publication 946 and Notice 2026-11: depreciation eligibility and bonus-depreciation guidance.
