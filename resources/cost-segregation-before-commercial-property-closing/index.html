Cost Segregation Before a Commercial Property Closing: Buyer Due Diligence

A business owner buying an office, clinic, warehouse or other commercial building can investigate cost segregation before closing. The useful pre-closing outcome is a documented review plan, not a deduction promised from the listing price. This guide explains what to request while the seller and transaction team still have the records readily available.

Identify the purchaser and intended use

Confirm which legal entity will own the property and which entity will occupy it. Direct use by an operating business and a separately owned building leased to that business require different analysis. Put the ownership chart, lease plan and expected occupants in front of the tax adviser before relying on an estimated tax benefit.

Identify personal, exempt, vacant or mixed-use areas rather than assuming the entire building has identical treatment. The IRS depreciation publication describes depreciable ownership, business or income-producing use, basis and placed-in-service requirements. It also explains that land is not depreciable. A purchase-price total does not answer those questions.

Request records while negotiating the transaction

  • Available construction plans, specifications and improvement invoices.
  • Seller's existing study and asset records, if the seller will provide them.
  • Property inspection information, photographs and details of major systems.
  • Draft allocation, appraisal and closing-cost information for adviser review.
  • Dates and budgets for renovations, occupancy and readiness for intended use.

Do not represent a seller's study as the buyer's completed study. The transaction may establish different basis and timing, and the property may have changed. Ask the provider what can be reused as evidence and what needs fresh verification. Obtain any permissions necessary to inspect the building or receive records through the transaction team.

Separate an early estimate from the final report

A feasibility estimate can help decide whether further work is worth considering. It should identify what is known and what remains provisional, including the closing basis, land allocation, available cost detail and actual use. Ask whether the fee covers a preliminary review, a completed study or both, and whether the quote changes if the transaction does not close.

The IRS study audit guide describes evidence and analysis relevant to reviewing a study. It does not approve an individual provider. A provider should be able to explain its method for this property rather than relying on a universal allocation percentage or an audit-proof label.

Do not treat closing day as the automatic deduction date

Buying property and placing it in service are not necessarily the same event. A building or improvement must be ready and available for its intended use under the applicable rules. Ask the adviser what evidence supports the service date when renovation or conversion continues after closing. Do not move a deduction into a desired year merely because a payment was made.

Bonus depreciation, Section 179 and ordinary depreciation have separate eligibility requirements. A cost segregation report does not by itself establish that every shorter-lived asset qualifies for a particular allowance. Ask the preparer to apply the rules for the acquisition, service date, asset and taxpayer involved rather than copying a prior-year illustration.

Test usability and the exit plan before ordering

The owner-level tax analysis is separate from the engineering classification. Where the building is held in a rental entity, passive-loss and other limitations may matter even when the tenant is the owner’s business. IRS Publication 925 is a starting point for this discussion; it is not evidence that a particular self-rental loss can offset operating income.

Ask for a holding-period review if a sale, refinancing or business exit is likely. Accelerated deductions affect adjusted basis, and a later disposition can have tax consequences. The study quote should not be presented as a permanent tax saving without considering the applicable sale treatment. IRS Publication 544 explains relevant disposition and recapture concepts.

Assign the post-closing work before everyone moves on

Record who will obtain the final settlement statement, update the cost basis, finish the study, answer the preparer's questions and enter the supported depreciation. Agree on a deliverable list and document deadline. If an older asset or prior return is involved, ask for a separate correction-method analysis instead of assuming the new purchase procedure applies.

At completion, retain the final report, basis reconciliation, depreciation schedule and explanation of unresolved issues together. A good handoff lets a future preparer understand the work without rebuilding it from emails. Confirm whether ongoing questions and examination support are included in the engagement or billed separately.

Have a focused pre-closing advisory conversation

AE Tax Advisors publishes this guide for buyers preparing a professional review. Discuss the purchase and tax-planning scope before treating a promotional estimate as part of your acquisition budget. Give the property type, expected closing date and ownership structure without posting confidential documents in booking notes. The engagement should specify study work, return implementation and any separate transaction advice.
