Tenant Improvement Cost Segregation: Resolve Ownership and Scope Before Ordering

A business can spend heavily fitting out leased space without owning the building. Before commissioning cost segregation, determine which improvements belong in the taxpayer's review and how landlord payments or allowances affect the records. A proposal based only on the renovation budget can miss the most important question: whose assets and costs are being analyzed?

Start with the lease and the improvement agreement

Collect the lease, work letter, allowance provisions and amendments. Identify the party that contracted for the work, paid the invoices, received reimbursements and controls the relevant assets. Tell the tax adviser what happens to the improvements when the lease ends and whether any equipment can be removed.

The tax analysis should follow the actual legal and economic arrangement. Do not assume that the name on one invoice, the party that initially advanced cash or the location of an asset settles every ownership question. Ask the adviser and attorney to resolve unclear lease terms before the study provider builds its final asset list.

Build a cost record that includes reimbursements

Prepare separate schedules for tenant-paid work, landlord-paid work, allowances, credits and disputed amounts. Reconcile contractor costs to the books while preserving how each payment was funded. If both parties have the same invoices, coordinate the scope so neither study silently treats the entire project as belonging to its own taxpayer.

Give the adviser the reimbursement terms and payment dates, not just the net cash cost. Ask how those facts affect the applicable basis and reporting. A cost segregation report should reconcile to the tax basis approved for the taxpayer rather than assume that every dollar in the overall project budget can be depreciated by the tenant.

Do not label every fit-out cost qualified improvement property

Describe the actual work, building history and dates. Interior changes, equipment, building systems and other expenditures may require different analysis. Qualified improvement property is a tax category with requirements and exclusions; the phrase tenant improvement in a contract is not enough to establish it.

IRS Publication 946 discusses depreciation and qualified improvement property. Ask the preparer to identify which rules apply to each relevant category and tax year. Avoid a proposal that promises one treatment for the entire renovation without examining the underlying facts.

Separate capitalization from the study's classifications

Some project invoices may involve repairs, replacements or other work requiring a capitalization review. Give the adviser the pre-work condition, purpose of the project and detailed scope. Do not assume that ordering cost segregation decides whether an expenditure should have been capitalized in the first place.

The IRS tangible property guidance explains the framework for amounts paid to acquire, produce or improve property. Agree on who addresses that question before the study provider reconciles the final asset costs. Keep any material assumptions visible in the report handoff.

Plan the delivery and later lease events

  • Identify the taxpayer, premises and improvement scope covered.
  • Document ownership and allowance questions resolved by the advisers.
  • Confirm the supported basis and relevant placed-in-service dates.
  • Assign study delivery, tax-return implementation and record retention.
  • Preserve asset detail for later replacement, relocation or lease termination.
  • Specify what post-report questions and revisions the provider will support.
