Comparing Cost Segregation Proposals for a Property Portfolio

A portfolio owner can receive a persuasive total-savings estimate without knowing which property justifies the study cost. Before commissioning several cost segregation studies at once, ask providers and your tax adviser to separate property-level work from owner-level tax use. This guide provides a purchasing framework for business owners with several rentals or commercial buildings.

Build a property schedule before requesting a package quote

For each property, record its legal owner, use, acquisition date, placed-in-service history, purchase or construction basis, prior improvements, existing study status and anticipated sale date. Identify which return reports the property and whether the same preparer handles every entity. Use a separate row for each property rather than a portfolio total that blends unrelated owners and tax years.

The schedule can reveal an inexpensive first step: obtaining missing depreciation records before buying a new study. A provider cannot reliably compare historical deductions with a revised classification if the original basis and improvements are not reconciled. Existing studies also need to be identified so the proposed work does not charge for unnecessary duplication.

Compare scope at the property level

Ask each provider to identify the properties covered, evidence-gathering method, report format, treatment of incomplete records and review process. A portfolio discount is not meaningful if one quote includes an inspection and reconciliation while another assumes the owner's spreadsheet is final. Compare the documented work, not the marketing label assigned to the service.

The IRS Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide discusses study methodology, documentation and report quality. It is guidance for examination, not an IRS endorsement of any provider or a guarantee that a report will be accepted. Request support for the specific assumptions that drive your property results.

Keep deduction estimates separate from usable tax benefits

A study identifies potential depreciation treatment; the owner’s return determines how a deduction is used. Basis, at-risk, passive-activity and other limitations can change timing. A business owner with substantial income should not assume every rental loss offsets that income. IRS Publication 925 explains why the activity and owner facts matter.

Ask your tax adviser for at least two cases: the anticipated use of deductions and a case in which some remain suspended. A large first-year number should be labeled as a deduction, not a cash saving, unless the calculation actually includes the relevant tax assumptions. For shared ownership, the analysis may need to distinguish partners instead of assigning everyone the same result.

Use a proposal comparison worksheet

  • Study cost: price per property and any package discount, with scope changes specified.
  • Implementation: separate fee for return work, accounting-method analysis and amended-return or other procedures if applicable.
  • Records: documents supplied, gaps, reconciliation work and who resolves them.
  • Timing: study delivery and filing milestones, each conditioned on complete information.
  • Support: what questions, revisions and examination assistance are included, and for how long.
  • Future events: records available for dispositions, improvements and a change of preparer.

Request the assumptions in writing and give competing providers the same property facts. If an estimate changes because a property has unusual improvements, mixed use or poor records, that is a reason to investigate scope rather than simply choose the lowest number.

Decide which studies should happen first

Consider documentation readiness, supported incremental deductions, ability to use the deductions, total implementation cost and the planned holding period. These are decision inputs, not a universal ranking formula. A property scheduled for sale may need a disposition model before a new study is ordered; a property with missing records may require cleanup first.

For a fictional three-property portfolio, the largest building may have the highest gross depreciation estimate while a smaller property has complete records and a simpler filing path. The correct purchasing sequence depends on the entire review. It should not be based only on square footage, purchase price or a provider's projected percentage.

Document the handoff to the return preparer

Agree on one owner for the implementation schedule. Ask who will reconcile the final report to the books, determine the filing approach and deliver updated asset records. IRS Publication 946 distinguishes depreciation corrections and accounting-method changes. A study provider's finished PDF is not evidence that the tax return has been implemented.
