New Construction Cost Segregation: Define the Study Scope Before Final Draws

A new building gives a cost segregation provider access to records that may be hard to recover later: plans, contractor cost detail, change orders and information from the people who built it. Before the project closes out, agree on what the study provider and tax adviser need. The goal is a reconciled asset record, not simply a deduction estimate based on the construction budget.

Start with the contract and the actual scope

Give the provider the construction contract, schedule of values, plans and approved changes. Identify items supplied directly by the owner and work performed under separate contracts. A general contractor's total may not include equipment, site work, professional services or later additions paid outside that contract.

The IRS Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide discusses documentation and methods used in evaluating studies. It is an examination guide, not an IRS approval of a provider or a substitute for current law. Ask the provider how the available actual-cost records will be used and which areas still require estimation.

Reconcile draws without counting the same cost twice

Keep a summary of original contract value, approved changes, amounts billed, amounts paid, retainage and credits. Distinguish a contractor's cumulative payment application from a new invoice amount. Owner bookkeeping, bank draws and the study should be reconciled to the same underlying project rather than added together as if they were separate costs.

For each difference, retain a short explanation and source reference. A lender's draw is financing evidence; it does not by itself determine the tax classification of the work. Ask who resolves disputed or incomplete costs before the final report is issued.

Track owner-paid and indirect costs separately

Create a file for architect and engineering work, permits, project management, financing-related items and other costs outside the main construction invoices. The tax adviser should determine which amounts belong in depreciable basis, land, another asset category or a different treatment. The study scope should say who makes those decisions and how the final totals reconcile.

Do not assume that every cash outflow during construction receives the same depreciation treatment. Give the adviser the actual invoices and purpose of the expenditure. A category named miscellaneous is a signal to investigate, not a tax classification.

Document when the property is ready for use

Record completion and use milestones with their supporting evidence. Include inspections, occupancy documentation, tenant or operating commencement and any separate phases. Ask which facts establish the relevant placed-in-service dates. A funding date, purchase order or final contractor payment is not automatically the controlling date.

IRS Publication 946 explains placed-in-service and depreciation principles. The adviser should apply them to the actual property. If part of the project remains unfinished while another part begins operating, flag that fact rather than forcing every asset into a single date for convenience.

Specify the study and return handoff

  • A reconciled total that connects the study to the approved tax basis.
  • An organized asset schedule with descriptions and classification support.
  • A record of estimation methods and unresolved limitations.
  • A process for later change orders, credits or corrected invoices.
  • Named responsibility for implementing the report in the tax return.
  • Records that can support later improvements and dispositions.

Ask whether the proposal includes a preliminary review during construction, a final study, or both. Clarify what triggers additional fees if the scope expands. Compare deliverables and documentation, not only the percentage of cost projected into shorter-lived categories.

Coordinate the project team early

AE Tax Advisors publishes this guide. Discuss the tax-review scope for a construction study with the property type, project stage and reporting deadline. Share detailed contracts and costs through the agreed secure process. The study's classifications and the owner's ability to use deductions are separate questions, and no result is guaranteed by assembling the file.
