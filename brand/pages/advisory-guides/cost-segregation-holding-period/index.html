Evaluate cost segregation across the expected holding period

A study can improve near-term cash flow while creating different tax timing later. A holding-period model makes the future assumptions explicit and shows whether the result remains useful if plans change.

By AE Tax Advisors · Updated October 10, 2026

The decisionRecords to prepareTrade-offsImplementationQuestions for your adviser

How does the timing benefit compare with fees, future deductions, and disposition consequences?

The value of earlier deductions depends on when they can be used and what occurs later. The owner’s tax rate, applicable limitations, asset classifications, state treatment, and sale assumptions can all affect the comparison.

Illustrative situation

An owner expects to hold a property for ten years but receives a purchase offer in year three. A first-year-only analysis does not show what the shorter horizon changes. Compare the same property under no study, a longer hold, and an earlier sale, using the same operating assumptions so the tax timing difference remains visible.

Prepare a file the reviewer can trace

Keep the original documents and use a summary to connect them. Note the relevant owner, entity, property, and reporting period. Distinguish confirmed facts from estimates and unresolved questions.

Compare the trade-offs

Present-value analysis recognizes that a dollar today and a dollar later are different, but a chosen discount rate is still an assumption. Report the undiscounted annual cash flows alongside any present-value result. Do not describe a timing benefit as permanent tax elimination or ignore the baseline deductions that would have occurred without the study.

Make implementation explicit

Ask the tax professional to identify which figures are estimates and which are supported by current records. Save the model date, assumptions, and source report version. Revisit the model when an owner’s income, property use, financing, or sale plan changes. A model supports a decision; it does not replace the filed return analysis.

Questions for your adviser

  • Is the comparison against the existing depreciation schedule?
  • Are limitations modeled before calling a deduction a cash benefit?
  • What future tax effects are included?
  • How does an earlier sale change the result?

Primary guidance and review boundaries

IRS Publication 544: Sales and other dispositions of assets provides the underlying federal framework. Confirm the applicable tax year, current source version, state treatment, and your own facts with the responsible professional. This guide organizes a decision; it does not establish your tax treatment.
