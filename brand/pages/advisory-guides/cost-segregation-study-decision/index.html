Decide whether a cost segregation study belongs in your plan

The first-year deduction is one part of the decision. A complete comparison includes usable tax benefit, study and filing costs, state treatment, the holding period, and what happens when the property is sold.

By AE Tax Advisors · Updated October 10, 2026

The decisionRecords to prepareTrade-offsImplementationQuestions for your adviser

Does changing depreciation timing improve the owner’s overall position?

Cost segregation identifies property components for tax classification and recovery-period analysis. It does not independently determine whether an owner can use the resulting deduction or whether the investment is attractive.

Illustrative situation

Two owners consider a study on similar buildings. One expects taxable income that may absorb eligible deductions; the other has limitations that may defer use. The buildings may produce similar classification results while the owners experience different benefit timing. A report estimate must be connected with the owner’s tax projection.

Prepare a file the reviewer can trace

Keep the original documents and use a summary to connect them. Note the relevant owner, entity, property, and reporting period. Distinguish confirmed facts from estimates and unresolved questions.

Compare the trade-offs

A study may move deductions earlier rather than permanently eliminate tax. State treatment can differ from federal treatment, and disposition can change the lifetime result. Compare no study, study now, and study later using realistic cash-flow assumptions. A single marginal-rate multiplication cannot capture all of those differences.

Make implementation explicit

Have the return preparer review the proposed scope and ownership facts before commissioning the study. Confirm how the report will reconcile with the existing asset schedule and whether a method-change procedure is involved. Agree on who resolves classification questions and provides support if another preparer takes over later.

Questions for your adviser

  • What is the modeled benefit after costs and limitations?
  • Is the land allocation and depreciable cost pool supported?
  • What changes if the property is sold sooner?
  • Who implements the report and retains the supporting file?

Primary guidance and review boundaries

IRS Publication 946: How to depreciate property provides the underlying federal framework. Confirm the applicable tax year, current source version, state treatment, and your own facts with the responsible professional. This guide organizes a decision; it does not establish your tax treatment.
