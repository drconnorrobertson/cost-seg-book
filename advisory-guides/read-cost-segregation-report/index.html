Read a cost segregation report from basis to implementation

A polished report should allow the reviewer to trace the property facts, cost pool, methodology, classification, and final schedules. Begin with reconciliation before relying on the deduction summary.

By AE Tax Advisors · Updated October 10, 2026

The decisionRecords to prepareTrade-offsImplementationQuestions for your adviser

Can the preparer trace the report’s conclusions to the property and its records?

Engineering descriptions, cost estimates, and tax classifications serve different purposes. The report should explain the source and limits of each. A component label without its function, cost support, and classification reasoning is difficult to evaluate.

Illustrative situation

A report lists several shorter-lived components, but its total includes appliances purchased separately and a building amount that differs from the preparer’s schedule. The reviewer should resolve the cost bridge before implementing the new categories. Otherwise, an apparently detailed report can duplicate basis or begin from the wrong amount.

Prepare a file the reviewer can trace

Keep the original documents and use a summary to connect them. Note the relevant owner, entity, property, and reporting period. Distinguish confirmed facts from estimates and unresolved questions.

Property factsAddress, use, owner, dates, and inspection informationDoes the report describe the actual asset and reporting period?

Method and supportPlans, photographs, invoices, estimates, and classification explanationCan the reviewer understand how each material conclusion was reached?

Compare the trade-offs

A longer report is not necessarily a more reliable report. Detail matters when it makes the analysis traceable. Estimates can be necessary, but their source and limitations should remain visible. Ask how material uncertainty changes the conclusion instead of treating a large page count or a branded methodology as proof of accuracy.

Make implementation explicit

Create an exception list for missing records, differences in basis, disputed components, and implementation questions. Assign each item to the study provider or return preparer and retain written responses. Keep the accepted report version, final tax schedules, and supporting records together so a later sale or inquiry can be addressed without reconstructing the analysis.

Questions for your adviser

  • Does the report reconcile to the original asset schedule?
  • Are land and previously recorded assets excluded appropriately?
  • What supports component function and allocated cost?
  • Which open questions prevent implementation?

Primary guidance and review boundaries

IRS Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide provides the underlying federal framework. Confirm the applicable tax year, current source version, state treatment, and your own facts with the responsible professional. This guide organizes a decision; it does not establish your tax treatment.
