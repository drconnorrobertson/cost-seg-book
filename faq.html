Frequently Asked Questions

Updated November 21, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

Questions to resolve before commissioning a study or filing its results

What is a cost segregation study?

A study documents how a property’s depreciable basis is allocated among assets with different tax treatments. Its value depends on supported classifications and the owner’s ability to use the deductions. Ask for a report that reconciles to the property’s total basis and explains the evidence behind each allocation. The IRS Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide (February 2025) describes study approaches and review issues; it is examination guidance, not legal authority or approval of a particular report.

How much does a cost segregation study cost?

Obtain a written, property-specific quote rather than relying on an advertised average. Ask whether the fee covers site work, report revisions, tax-preparer coordination, an accounting-method change and responses to questions after delivery. Compare proposals using the same property list and deliverables. See the multi-property proposal comparison guide and AE engagement scope and fees.

What types of properties should be reviewed?

Start with the property’s actual business or income-producing use, ownership, basis and asset records. A building’s listing category or purchase price does not establish that a study will be worthwhile. Identify the decision first: a new acquisition, completed construction, tenant improvements or a review of existing depreciation. Bring that scope to the provider before authorizing work.

Can a study review a property I already own?

Yes, but the filing route needs a separate review. An accounting-method change may require Form 3115 and a Section 481(a) adjustment; it is not an unconditional right to claim every past deduction immediately. Have the preparer determine the applicable procedure, eligibility, deadlines and treatment of prior returns. Consult the Form 3115 instructions and current automatic-change guidance.

Does 100% bonus depreciation apply to every reclassified asset?

No. IRS Publication 946 explains the reinstated 100% allowance for certain qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Acquisition timing, asset eligibility, elections and other rules still matter. A study date does not replace the asset’s acquisition or placed-in-service date. Ask the preparer to identify the applicable rule for each asset rather than applying one percentage to the entire report.

Does a study prevent or trigger an IRS audit?

No provider can establish your audit outcome simply by delivering a study. Ask how its report supports classifications, reconciles costs and handles assumptions or missing records. The IRS examination guide helps frame review questions, but following a guide is not an audit exemption or a guarantee that an allocation will be accepted.

How does a study differ from the building’s depreciation schedule?

The building and its components may have different recovery periods and methods. A study supplies a supported allocation; the preparer applies the tax rules, conventions and any elections. Under general MACRS rules, residential rental and nonresidential real property commonly use 27.5- and 39-year periods, respectively, but alternative-system and property-specific rules can change treatment. Consult Publication 946; do not assume that a full annual amount applies in the first year.

How long does a cost segregation study take?

Ask for a written schedule tied to the actual scope and filing deadline. Identify when records must be delivered, whether access to the property is needed, when a draft will be reviewed and who will resolve preparer questions. Do not assume the report delivery date also completes the tax filing work. Agree how missing records or scope changes affect the schedule.

Is there a universal minimum property value?

A single purchase-price cutoff does not show whether a study pays for itself. Compare the quoted cost with the timing of deductions you can actually use, expected holding period, later sale consequences and state treatment. A high headline deduction can have little current value when the resulting loss is suspended. Require a comparison using your facts before deciding.

What documents should I gather?

Prepare the closing statement, basis allocation, construction and renovation invoices, available plans, asset list, prior depreciation schedules and relevant photographs. Label missing items instead of replacing them with unsupported totals. Ask the provider which assumptions remain unresolved and how the final report reconciles to the tax records. Use the report-reading checklist to organize the handoff.

What changes when I sell the property?

Review the sale asset by asset. Section 1245 ordinary-income recapture and Section 1250 treatment differ; recovery period alone does not establish the recapture category. Unrecaptured Section 1250 gain is distinct from ordinary-income recapture and can have a maximum 25% federal rate for individuals. See Publication 544. A Section 1031 exchange is limited to qualifying real property and does not automatically defer every asset’s gain or recapture. Model sale proceeds, allocation and replacement property before assuming deferral.

Can the resulting loss offset my W-2 income?

A depreciation deduction is not automatically a usable loss. Publication 925 explains participation, passive-activity and at-risk rules; other limits can also apply. An average customer-use period of seven days or less can affect Section 469 classification, but does not by itself prove material participation or a deductible wage offset. Ask for a complete return comparison, including suspended losses, rather than multiplying the study’s deduction by your highest tax rate.

Does real estate professional status make every rental loss deductible?

No. Publication 925 describes the more-than-750-hours and more-than-half-of-personal-services tests in qualifying real-property businesses in which the taxpayer materially participates. On a joint return, one spouse must separately meet those qualification tests. Material participation in the relevant rental activity still needs review; status alone does not remove all loss limits. Bring property-level records and any grouping elections to the preparer.

Is cost segregation only for new properties?

No. Existing property can also be reviewed, but “older” does not mean “larger benefit.” Start with its remaining basis, prior deductions, ownership history and available documentation. Ask whether a prospective schedule, a correction or an accounting-method review is needed. Do not commission a report on the assumption that the filing result is already determined.

How can I review the handoff to my CPA?

Ask who checks total basis, asset classifications, placed-in-service dates, prior depreciation and the filing procedure. Request a list of proposed changes with the source documents that support them. Confirm responsibility for entering the final schedule and retaining the report. If the provider and preparer disagree, resolve that issue before filing rather than leaving two incompatible schedules in the property file.

What if depreciation rules change?

Keep the acquisition, construction, service-date and election records needed to apply the rules for the affected year. Re-run the model when the law or your facts change. A shorter recovery period can change timing, but that does not guarantee a better after-tax result after fees, loss limits and eventual disposition. Ask which inputs would change the recommendation.
